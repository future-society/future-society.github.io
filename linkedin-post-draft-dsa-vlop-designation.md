## Draft post text

The DSA's strictest tier just got three new members — and one of them isn't a "platform" in the traditional sense at all.

On 31 August, the European Commission designated ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine under the Digital Services Act — a first for an AI chatbot — after OpenAI reported over 159 million average monthly users in the EU. Reddit and Roblox were designated Very Large Online Platforms the same day. All three now face the DSA's toughest obligations: systemic-risk assessments covering illegal content, minor safety, elections and fundamental rights, independent audits, and researcher data access — with fines of up to 6% of global turnover for non-compliance.

This is exactly the terrain our Systemic Risk Mitigation focus area works in. At Future Society Hub, we build techno-legal tools to identify and mitigate the systemic risks of dominant online platforms — including auditing how platforms like Meta and TikTok have handled researcher data-access duties under DSA Article 40. Extending VLOP-style scrutiny to a conversational AI system raises a genuinely new question: what does a "systemic risk assessment" even mean for a search engine that generates its answers rather than ranking them? The DSA's transparency and audit machinery was designed for platforms sorting content — now regulators, researchers and companies alike have four months to work out how it applies to one that authors it.

That's a research question as much as a compliance one — and precisely where interdisciplinary teams belong: legal scholars and computer scientists, working alongside regulators, at the University of St.Gallen and beyond.

Should generative AI search engines be regulated like platforms, like search engines, or as something new entirely? 👇
