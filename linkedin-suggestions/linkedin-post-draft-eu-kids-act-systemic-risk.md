## Draft post text

The European Commission hasn't officially unveiled the EU Kids Act yet — but the draft already leaked, and it changes who has to prove what.

Reported this week by Euronews, MLex and others: the plan would bar under-3s from social media, video platforms and AI chatbots outright; limit 3–13-year-olds to supervised, child-friendly accounts; give 13–15-year-olds restricted accounts; and set 15 as the age for full, independent access. It names Meta's Facebook and Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit, X and OpenAI's ChatGPT directly, and it bans infinite scrolling, artificial notifications and engagement-maximizing rewards outright.

The most interesting part isn't the age gates — attempts at those already exist. It's the governance switch underneath: very large platforms must submit compliance plans and get a Commission opinion before shipping any new feature that could affect minors, with the burden of proof on the platform rather than on regulators to catch violations after the fact.

That switch runs straight into a problem our Alice Palmieri and Aurelia Tamò-Larrieux, with Konrad Kollnig (Maastricht University), mapped in their scoping review "Systemic risks of dominant online platforms" (Computer Law & Security Review, 2026): the evidence base for assessing platform systemic risk from outside the platform is still remarkably thin. Asking a platform to certify its own compliance only works if someone else can meaningfully check the claim — and FuSo's own audit of Meta's and TikTok's DSA research-data channels found those channels already strip most of the metadata an outside auditor would need to do exactly that.

Good law on paper still needs a verification layer underneath it. That's what Systemic Risk Mitigation means at Future Society Hub — FuSo, hosted at the University of St.Gallen — and it's also why our flag&safe project works with schools directly: regulation reshapes what platforms are allowed to build, but young people are navigating these products today, drafts and all.

To anyone inside DSA enforcement or child-safety policy: does self-certified compliance work here, or does the EU Kids Act need its own Article 40(12) moment before it's finalized?
