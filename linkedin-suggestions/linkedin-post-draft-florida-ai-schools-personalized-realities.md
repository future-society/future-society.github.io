## Draft post text

Same week, two U.S. school systems drew opposite lines around AI in the classroom.

On 17 September, the Florida Board of Education passed statewide guidelines for K-12 AI use: parents must be notified and give consent before their child uses an AI product, and schools must offer a non-AI alternative of equal quality to anyone who opts out. AI tools are explicitly barred from simulating emotional relationships with students, from behavior monitoring, social scoring, or psychological profiling. No unsupervised use. Vendors can't sell or train their own models on student data. Commissioner Henry Mack called it keeping "Florida in front, rather than reacting."

The same week, New York City Public Schools and Los Angeles Unified — the two largest districts in the country — went the other way and banned student AI use outright for 2026–27.

Our Jannis Strecker, with Simon Mayer and Kenan Bektaş, argued in "Towards Societally Beneficial Personalized Realities" (ACM DIS 2025, Honorable Mention) that neither extreme is the real fix. Their paper names what unconstrained personalization does at scale — a loss of shared worlds, the emergence of perceptual filter bubbles — which is close to a research description of exactly what Florida is now trying to legislate against by name: AI that profiles, monitors, or emotionally simulates its way into a child's decisions. But the paper's answer isn't prohibition either. It sketches a conceptual foundation for personalization systems designed to be beneficial to the individual and to society, not just less harmful than the current default.

A ban sidesteps the design question. A consent form doesn't answer it either — it just moves the burden onto parents to evaluate systems that are, by design, hard to evaluate from the outside. The harder, more useful work is what Personalized Realities is built for: specifying what a classroom AI tool would actually have to look like, architecturally, to be trustworthy for a child by default.

This is what "Education & Digital Literacy" means in our Focus Areas at Future Society Hub — FuSo, hosted at the University of St.Gallen.

To educators and policymakers: does your state's approach ban personalization for students, require consent for it, or actually redesign it?
